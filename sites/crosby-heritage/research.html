Sources, leads, conflicts

Research and records

Confirmed means a record states it. Probable means the records point to it and a certificate would make it firmer. Family tradition is labeled as such. A lead or a possible claim is listed here and is not drawn on the tree or the map.

Open leads

  • George Magwire and Mary (Prentice) Magwire, named in a Find a Grave note as the parents of Albert A. Magwire. No record has been seen. A Brooklyn birth also points away from the Brandon, Vermont, Magwire family (John Magwire and Francis G. Magwire), which is a separate unlinked lead.
  • Nathaniel Spalding, named in the Spalding Memorial (1872) as the father of Charles Elliot Spaulding, and the longer pedigree in that book back toward Edward Spalding. Compiled genealogy only.
  • Timothy Putnam of Charlestown, New Hampshire, and Betsey (Hall) Putnam, named in the Dickey Genealogy (1898) as the parents of Amantha Putnam. The book gives Betsey a birth on May 26, 1784. Compiled genealogy only.
  • Whether Charles Pierce, named as Eva's father in 1906, is the Charles Pierce who married Alice Whitehouse at Hebron, Yarmouth County, in 1865. Eva's mother Alice may have had the maiden name Whitehouse. Both are unproved. The 1865 register.
  • What became of Laura Mary (Seward) Farr after the 1912 marriage: death or divorce, and when Harry James Farr married Mary Abbie West. An "Abbie West" was paid for teaching in a Chester, Vermont, report dated 1927. If that is Mary Abbie, the marriage was about 1926 or 1927. The Chester report.
  • Whether the Harry James Farr who married in Springfield in 1912 is the Harry J. Farr, born in Colorado about 1888, in the 1950 census. Same name, same town, and the age fits. Still probable.
  • The 1882 marriage index reads James H. Crosby. Ernest's 1906 marriage says James W. The 1937 death registration says James William. One man is the best reading. A census of James, Asenath and Ernest in one household would close it.
  • Family tradition: summer visits to a West farm in Wells, Maine. The place table marks it possible, so it is not on the map.

Conflicts

The husband's name

The tree follows the research files and calls Tracy's husband Richard "Rick" R. Crosby. Roy E. Crosby's 2014 obituary and Dorothy's 2022 obituary list their children as Robert, Debra, Rick and Lynn. Howard Magwire's memorial says "Tracy Crosby (Richard)". Gwendolyn (Farr) Magwire's 2009 obituary names her son-in-law as Richard Crosby. A family account has also used the name Robert Richardson Crosby for Tracy's husband. The records point to Richard, called Rick, and to Robert as his brother. The middle initial R. may stand for Richardson, his mother's maiden name, which is not proved. The question stays open until Sebastian or the family says, or until a marriage record is read. No addresses or other personal details are published.

Gwendolyn's mother

Largely settled, and still short of a civil record. Gwendolyn was born in 1927, so in 1950 she was in her twenties and not in her parents' household. Richard H. Farr's obituary names the parents as Harry James Farr and Mary Abbie West Farr and lists sisters Alma and Gwendolyn. Laura Mary Seward, married in 1912, was Harry's first wife. When Harry and Mary Abbie married, and what became of Laura, are the open points above.

Spellings

Magwire and Maguire, and Spalding and Spaulding, are spellings, not two families. Ethel is memorialized as Maguire. Edna's death index says Albert Maguire. The 1872 book spells Spalding. The family used Spaulding. Search both.

Barbara's birth day

Closed. A computer reading of the 1921 Wayland register said October 20. The printed page, and her obituary, say October 26, 1921. The page image is on the Richardson page.

Not yet on the map

The map is driven only by the research place table. A place known only roughly (a Nova Scotia village, a cemetery plot we have not measured, the state of Colorado) is a soft circle or an area, not a street pin. The map's own list, "Not yet on the map," gathers dated facts that have no coordinate of their own: a death whose pin is the burial, Bernard and Beatrice's 1948 marriage, Roy's Navy service, and similar gaps.

Named in the records, and not given a pin:

  • Pleasant Lake, Nova Scotia, given as Joseph Crosby's birthplace. Pleasant Valley, where James was born, is on the map. The two names are not treated as one place.
  • Lake George, Nova Scotia, Eunice (Saunders) Crosby's birthplace, and South Ohio, Celia Porter's birthplace. Ohio itself is on the map for the 1882 marriage and for Asenath's birth.
  • Otis Reservoir, the summer home in Roy and Dorothy's obituaries. Family tradition.
  • Montpelier, Vermont, where Frank Magwire belonged to Mt. Sinai Shrine.
  • Wells, Maine, and Chester, Vermont, the leads above.
  • Hebron, Nova Scotia, the unproved 1865 Pierce marriage, and Brandon, Vermont, the unlinked Magwire memorials.
  • Howard Magwire's stay in West Springfield by 1967. Roy and Dorothy's home there is on the map. Howard's is not a separate pin.

Held back

  • Living people: name and relationship only. No dates, places, photographs or biographies, and no map pins. That covers Tracy, Rick, Brittany, Mitch, Travis and Alma, and it covers Nellie Magwire, Doris Ann Elliott, Rick's siblings, and anyone named only as a living cousin.
  • No contact details for anyone.
  • Full obituary texts, funeral-home photographs, and Find a Grave photographs are not in this site. The obituaries of Richard H. Farr, Gwendolyn (Farr) Magwire and Barbara R. Vogel are linked. The page images stay in the private research files.
  • Gwendolyn's guest book, which names living cousins, is not used.
  • Seller scans and other third-party pictures are not in the repository. Town registers, census pages, the two genealogies and the Nova Scotia registrations are public records and are shown with the entry marked.
